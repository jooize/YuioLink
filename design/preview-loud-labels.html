YuioLink · design note 19

Loud labels

The italic was a whisper; the pill is the same sentence at a readable volume. Round 15's tags failed for a specific reason — they were judgements ("identifies", "sent as you") shouting in uppercase what the notes say better. What they had right was the costume: a pill is unmistakably a piece of UI, not a piece of the stored string. So the round-18 voice rule keeps its meaning and changes clothes: our labels (to, user, port, path) become sentence-case pills; literal keys (?cc, &subject, ;sid) stay bare mono — they are quotes and need no costume. The comma still sits outside the pill: it is the URL's character, not ours.

Both placements are mocked below. My call is the key column: labels and keys are the same kind of answer ("what is this row?") and belong in the same place; the trailing tag re-creates the empty-key hole that made join look broken, and on a long wrapped value it dangles mid-text where the eye reads it as part of the value.

And spotify on one line: keep it. The difference from the other cards is earned, not arbitrary — that headline is the stored string, verbatim, so it may sit as one run (and copy as one string); tel and mailto keep the separate plate because their headlines are formatted renderings. On a narrow screen the run wraps after spotify: and looks exactly like everyone else; on a wide one it earns its single line by being true.

The honest rendering of the trailing-tag idea, faults included: the first row's key cell is empty (the hole that made join look broken), the second row's lone comma dangles, and the two to tags sit at different distances from the left edge, so the eye cannot scan "what is each row?" down one column anymore. On a value long enough to wrap, the tag lands mid-paragraph. It also mixes registers — a UI pill living inside the value's mono text.
