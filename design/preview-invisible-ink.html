Invisible ink — the cast list, character only

Changes from note 27, per your feedback. The neighbour-context tiles are gone — just the character. Each entry is now: symbol · name · U+ · stored form · prose. The symbol wears the dotted square (the Unicode-chart dress for characters you cannot see), and beside it sits one plain field holding the actual character — which appears empty, on purpose: the emptiness is the demonstration. A printable escape like & is its own proof, so it gets one plain tile and no pair. Also on this page: the blue lid you picked is implemented (the fold below uses production CSS), and section 2 is the on-device check for the "maybe" receipt.

Quietest version. The dotted square says "this one you cannot see", the plain field beside it proves it, and the ZWSP entry says so outright ("the empty box is the character") so the blank never reads as a rendering bug. The ampersand needs no pair — the glyph is its own proof.

Self-labelling, at a cost. The captions make the pair impossible to misread on first contact, but they add a second line of type to every entry and the words repeat down the column. If the prose sentence in variant 1 ("The empty box is the character") does the job, the captions are ballast.

Recommendation. Variant 1. The dotted square is doing the explaining — it is the same dress every character-picker and Unicode chart uses for invisibles — and one plain sentence covers the one entry where the blank could puzzle. Rules it encodes:
  • Invisible character → dotted-square symbol + plain field with the real character (empty on purpose).
  • Printable-but-escaped character → one plain tile with the glyph.
  • Every entry: name, U+XXXX, the stored escape, where it sits. Repeats collapse to one entry with a count.
  • Red only where the chip is red (the hidden character); housekeeping escapes stay in the grey register.
