Share card — mark, legibility, foot copy

The card server/src/card.rs rasterises for og:image, at 1200×630. Three decisions are separated below so each can be judged on its own: what sits beside the wordmark, how dark and how large the grey text runs, and what the foot line says. Everything not under discussion in a section is held at its shipped value.

1 · The mark

The blue square is 30×30 at y=92 while the wordmark's cap height spans roughly y=88 to y=117 — so the square sits about 5px low and hangs below the baseline. Option A just fixes that. B and C drop the shape: one keeps a colour accent inside the wordmark, the other goes fully monochrome and lets the arrow be the only blue on the card.

At the size a chat client shows it

Link previews are rendered small — this row is 260px wide, close to what iMessage and Discord give an unopened card.

2 · Legibility of the grey text

The kicker and foot are #6e6e73 on the near-white card — about 4.6:1, AA at those sizes but the first thing to disappear when the card is scaled down. Darkening to #55555c (≈6.5:1) and nudging both up two points costs nothing else on the card. Shown here on option C.

3 · The foot line

What is certain at expiry: the link stops working, and the name goes back in the pool. What is not certain is that anyone claims it again — so "will change after" promises a new destination the name may never get. The options below split on which half they assert. The same wording should carry to the og:description prose in server/src/views.rs and the example in docs/PREVIEW.md.

Write your own

Rendered on option C with the darkened greys. The foot has room for roughly 46 characters at 33px before it reaches the right padding; the card does not wrap, so a longer line runs off the edge.

What each line claims

LineAssertsTrue?
may change afterThe destination might differ later.Accurate, but it undersells the certain part — this link is over on that date either way.
will change afterThe destination will differ later.Overclaims. If nobody takes the name, it resolves to nothing at all, which is not a change of destination.
never points here againThis name will not lead to this destination after the date.Certain, and it is the half that matters to whoever holds the link.
then the name is freeThe name returns to the pool.Certain, but it explains the mechanism rather than the consequence.
this link ends for goodThe link is finished.Certain. Says nothing about the name being reusable, which is the part that could surprise someone later.
