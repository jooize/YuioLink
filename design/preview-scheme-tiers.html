YuioLink · design note 4

Three tiers, by what pressing actually does

Also settled here: multi-use is dropped — one-time only, so the badge is just "Opens Once" and the unreachable "Limited Use" branches come out rather than staying as dead code.

1 · The line the tiers are drawn on

Not "web address / not a web address" — mailto: isn't one and is perfectly welcome. The question a visitor actually faces is what happens on my device when I press this, and there are exactly three answers.

Tier 1 — a website opens http, https.
Familiar, reversible, contained in the browser. One press, the amber Continue anchor.
Tier 2 — an app on your device acts mailto, tel, sms, ftp, magnet, spotify, xmpp, irc, ircs, matrix.
Leaves the browser's sandbox. Gets the cover.
Tier 3 — nothing, ever javascript, data, vbscript, and anything else off the allowlist.
Printed, never linked. No control at all.

Tier 2 earns its cover on the facts, not on a hunch. A tel: link can put a premium-rate number one accidental tap from being dialled — the classic charge-fraud vector on a phone, and the tap target on a phone is a thumb. A magnet: hands a payload to a torrent client that may start fetching before anyone reads anything. sms: can pre-fill a message to a shortcode. In every case the action lands outside the browser, where the back button does not reach. That is a different risk in kind from "this website might be junk", and it is the one place on this site where a deliberate second act is worth the friction.

3 · One colour for the tier, or one per scheme?

You suggested per-scheme colour. It looks good, and I think it breaks the rule that has been holding the palette together.

Pick

Tier colour, per-scheme icon

Amber throughout; the glyph carries the specifics
mailto:
tel:
magnet:

Colour on this site answers "where do I end up", and for every one of these the answer is the same: off YuioLink, into something we don't control. The decision is identical, so the colour is identical — and the icon, which answers the different question ("into what?"), is what varies. Ten schemes also means ten colours, several of which would have to be invented, and three of which already mean something else here (blue = stay, green = copied, purple = One-Time).

Against

Per-scheme colour

Mail blue, phone green, magnet purple
mailto:
tel:
magnet:

Prettier in a row like this, and worse on the page. A green tel: plate reads as safe — green means "copied, done, fine" everywhere else here — and tel: is the single most expensive mistake on the list. Per-scheme colour makes the palette describe the destination's brand instead of the visitor's risk.

4 · Is the cover worth it? The honest alternative

With cover

Two deliberate acts

tel:
+47 800 12 345
Dials a number — lift to continue Call +47 800 12 345

The cover's real value is not the extra click — it is that the lid states the consequence ("dials a number") in the place a button label would otherwise state the destination. A visitor who lifts it has read a verb.

Without

One press, consequence in the label

tel:
+47 800 12 345 Call +47 800 12 345

Simpler, and most of the protection is already here: the scheme plate is large, the verb is in the label, and nothing is one accidental tap from happening that was not also announced. If you decide the cover is too clever, this is the version I would ship — it is not a weak fallback.
