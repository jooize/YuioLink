Your questions



    
Does C lose stepping through the characters?

    
No. In C2 each chip has two parts: the name steps through that tier's entries, as the facts row does today, and the verb after it acts. The entry shows “2 of 4” and a close button. Stepping through the joiners shows their marks in blue even while they are hidden, so you can find them without turning them on.

    
Is holding the height needed for C?

    
Not for the controls, since they sit above the part that changes. One edge remains: when the page is scrolled to its very bottom and gets shorter, the browser scrolls up to fit, and everything shifts down. The hold would cover that too, and it costs little, so I would keep it in either option.

    
Other colors than red and gray?

    
Red stays: it means the character hides something. For the special characters, try the amber switch above. Amber already means “check this” in the domain note, but a no-break space or a soft hyphen is rarely a problem, and amber pulls the eye nearly as hard as red. I would keep gray (~65%). Joiners stay gray too: they start hidden, and blue is taken by the open entry. Green, rose and teal already mean removed, Public and Text.

    
Why “Removed” left the chip

    
“4 Hidden Characters Removed” is longer than the name it replaces, so the verb after it moved away from the pointer: sideways, or onto a new line on a phone. The name now stays the same, and the green check says the characters are out. With the verb's width also held, every control moves 0 px when clicked, measured at 1200 and 390 px.

    
Copy's sublabel

    
It names what is still in the text: “With Hidden and Special Characters”, then “With a Special Character” after one Remove, and “Without Hidden or Special Characters” once both are out. Joiners are never named, since they are spelling. A2 can carry the same sublabel.
