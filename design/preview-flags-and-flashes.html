1 · One renderer, every country's own punctuation

The tables dress each number themselves

libphonenumber international format, verbatim · dress A on top
🇸🇪Sweden +46 70-123 45 67
🇳🇴Norway +47 991 23 456
🇺🇸United States +1 415-555-2671
🇫🇷France +33 1 42 68 53 00
🇩🇪Germany +49 171 1234567

No per-country code in YuioLink, ever. Each region's format patterns — including whether a hyphen, a space, or nothing separates the groups — are data in libphonenumber's metadata, maintained by Google against each country's numbering plan. Sweden gets its hyphen, Norway its spaces, the US its hyphens, France its pairs, Germany its long run, all from format(number, International) verbatim. Dress A then colours what is already there: the country code recedes, separators go tertiary, national digits keep the bold. When the tables change, our rendering changes with the dependency bump — that staleness cost was priced in back in round 14.

Aligned at the seam, and every fact next to its number. The stack is a centred block with two columns inside: country codes right-aligned into the seam, national numbers all starting at the same x, tabular digits lining up beneath each other. Under each number, its own facts — and that is the payoff of your question: Premium Rate now points at the Norwegian number, not at the card. The chips shrink one step so the numbers stay the loudest thing. Single-number cards keep the centred chips row (nothing to disambiguate); the multi-number stack is where per-number placement earns its keep. Untick the premium number and its facts row dims with it — the warning leaves with the number it warned about.
