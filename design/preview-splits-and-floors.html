Splits and floors

Your round-24 review, point by point. Four corrections went straight into the gallery (preview-all-schemes): local parts back to regular weight (that was a regression against round 20's shading — good catch), a real gap after the ▸, no fold on the bare-path http card (a fold that can only restate one unactionable row earns nothing — new rule: the fold appears only when it has something to add), and the one-time card is now blind — you are right that the domain was leakage: it let anyone holding the link learn where it points without burning the use, and the burn is the whole tamper-evidence of a one-timer. Reveal now shows the full preview page — you spend the use to look, never to be thrown somewhere.

And you are right about mailto. RFC 6068 allows a to-less mailto — mailto:?cc=archive@records.example is legal and opens a compose like any other — so the floor moves from "last recipient" to "last address, anywhere": recipients and cc share one floor, and only the final address standing locks, whichever field it lives in. (Strictly, even mailto:?subject=hi is legal — zero addresses, blank compose. Keeping one address is a product choice, not a legal one: the button needs someone to name. Overrule it if you want the floor gone entirely.) Live in the gallery's merryGECKO card: untick both recipients and watch cc hold the floor.

The rest of your questions get their own sections below: the Copy split (1), Swedish punctuation and what tel:/sms: can carry (2–3), whether ftp and irc split (4–5), the refusal heading (6).

2 · Phones: the punctuation belongs to the tables

tel · Sweden — the hyphen is libphonenumber's, not ours
YuioLink Redirect polarELAND yuio.link

Exactly as stored tel:+46701234567 Call +46 70-123 45 67A phone number What opens it, if anything, is up to your device.

Expires in 6 days

Exactly your point, and the crate already carries it. Each country's punctuation lives in libphonenumber's own format strings — Sweden's national rendering is 070-123 45 67, hyphen included, from Google's tables, not from us. We render the international format verbatim, whatever characters the tables emit for that region. Real characters are fine in the headline now: it is declared a rendering, the exact line and its Copy button carry the stored truth, so nothing needs the round-13 margin trick anymore. One more grammar fact while we are here: a tel: URI is one number, always — RFC 3966 has no list form, so tel never grows the recipients treatment.

Yes, ftp splits — by the same rule as https, so usually not. The plain ftp://files.example.org/pub/notes.txt in the gallery stays a clean one-run card: nothing removable, nothing to add, no machinery. This one earns it: userinfo (same red, same chip as the web tier), a non-standard port (fixed — which server answers is not yours to change), and RFC 1738's ;type= transfer mode, a removable keyed tail. One rule everywhere: the machinery follows the parts, never the scheme.

Expires in 6 days

The formats, honestly: irc URLs were never standardised — the grammar is a de-facto draft (draft-butcher-irc-url) that clients follow loosely: irc[s]://host[:port]/[target][,flags][?key]. The target is a channel (the # is usually implied; when written it often arrives encoded as %23); ,isnick flags the target as a person, which would swap the subtitle to "A person on IRC"; ?key is a channel key. Classification accepts all of it, generates none of it. Splitting follows the parts rule: the plain card in the gallery has none and stays whole; this one has a fixed port, a fixed channel (the thing itself), and a removable ?key — dropping it still names the channel, your client just asks for the key instead of knowing it.

4 · The refusal heading — three candidates

Keep

No Button for This One

Names what the visitor notices

No Button for This OneThe address stored here would tell your browser to do something rather than go somewhere, so YuioLink shows it and stops there.

A fact about the page, verifiable by looking at it, and it answers the visitor's actual first question ("where's the button?"). The body then carries the why. Every heading on the site states; none of them judge — this one stays in character.

Alt

An Instruction, Not an Address

Names the reason instead

An Instruction, Not an AddressWhat is stored here would tell your browser to do something rather than go somewhere, so YuioLink shows it and stops there.

Also a pure fact — it fronts the diagnosis instead of the consequence. Slightly more to parse before the reader knows what it means for them; the body then half-repeats it. Viable, if you prefer the reason up top.

Against

Potentially Risky Instruction

A judgement, and a hedged one

Potentially Risky InstructionThe address stored here would tell your browser to do something rather than go somewhere, so YuioLink shows it and stops there.

This is the pattern the design has turned away three times: "Special" (a judgement without content), red buttons (asserting malice we can't know), green https (safety we can't promise). "Potentially" hedges and "Risky" judges — and a javascript: bookmarklet a friend shared is refused all the same, not because it is risky but because it is not an address. The refusal is structural; the heading should be too.

Nothing discloses before the spend — not the domain, not the scheme. The trade is honest because of what Reveal leads to: the full preview page (tiers, chips, slices, Continue), not the destination itself. You spend the use to look; nothing ever auto-opens. Symmetry bonus: a one-time Redirect now behaves exactly like a one-time Text — opaque until revealed, judged after.
