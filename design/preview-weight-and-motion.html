Weight, motion, hazard chips, phone numbers

1 · The lid no longer goes anywhere

The 3D flip is gone — no transform, no transform-origin, no perspective. Three ways to end the lid, none of which move it. Click each.

Pick

A · Instant

Lid is display: none when open tel: +47 800 12 345
Show…A phone number Call +47 800 12 345A phone number

The button is simply there, in the place the pointer already is. Nothing to wait for and nothing to watch. A cover is a gate, not an event.

B · Cross-fade

160ms opacity, no transform tel: +47 800 12 345
Show…A phone number Call +47 800 12 345A phone number

Softer, and the box never travels. Against: for 160ms both labels are legible at once, which is the one moment the control is ambiguous.

C · Colour arrives

Saturation and opacity together tel: +47 800 12 345
Show…A phone number Call +47 800 12 345A phone number

Reads as the same button waking up, which is the truest description of what happened. Against: the most machinery for the least difference, and it still has the double-label moment.

2 · Which part of a magnet deserves the weight?

Good question, and I think the intuitive answer is the wrong one.

Tempting

Bold the display name

magnet: ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3a94b81d7e05f2c6a9048bb3e1d7f4a2c60&dn=ubuntu-24.04-desktop-amd64.iso

dn is the least trustworthy string on the page. It is free text the creator typed — a label, not an identity. A magnet whose dn says ubuntu-24.04-desktop-amd64.iso can point at absolutely anything; the hash is what decides what arrives. Bolding it puts the page's strongest emphasis on the one field an attacker fully controls, on a page whose whole job is telling people which parts to believe.

Honest but unreadable

Bold the hash

magnet: ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3a94b81d7e05f2c6a9048bb3e1d7f4a2c60&dn=ubuntu-24.04-desktop-amd64.iso

Correct about what matters, useless in practice: nobody verifies forty hex characters by eye, and forty bold ones are harder to read than forty plain ones. Weight spent where it cannot be used.

Pick

Nothing bold — colour does it

magnet: ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3a94b81d7e05f2c6a9048bb3e1d7f4a2c60&dn=ubuntu-24.04-desktop-amd64.iso&tr=udp://tracker.example.org:6969

Three levels of colour are already three levels of hierarchy — keys quiet, values full, delimiters faint. Bold would be a fourth, and the only string that deserves it (the domain) is not present in a magnet at all. This keeps bold meaning exactly one thing site-wide: a registrable domain.

Bold the keys instead

magnet: ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3a94b81d7e05f2c6a9048bb3e1d7f4a2c60&dn=ubuntu-24.04-desktop-amd64.iso

Your other suggestion, and it does help long bodies break into readable chunks — xt, dn, tr become signposts. Against: it emphasises the scaffolding over the contents, and the keys are the one part of a magnet nobody needs to read.

The underlying rule, if you want one: a value is either an identifier (a hash, a number, an address — it is the thing) or a claim (a display name, a subject, a message body — free text the creator wrote about the thing). Identifiers can be verified; claims cannot. Nothing on this page should give a claim more visual authority than an identifier, which rules out bolding dn, subject and body — the three most tempting strings in the whole set.

3 · Hazard chips, and when they may fire

Same construction as "Not Encrypted": neutral pill, red symbol, words carrying the meaning. The rules matter more than the pills — a warning that fires on ordinary URLs is a warning nobody reads.

Fires

Padded with spaces

mailto: billing@example.com         attacker@evil.test
Padded with Spaces

Fires on a run of two or more spaces, or a leading or trailing one. A single space inside a subject line is ordinary and stays silent.

Fires

Hidden characters

mailto: hello@example.com?subject=Invoice%E2%80%AEfdp.exe
Hidden Characters

Fires only when an escape decodes to something invisible or direction-changing — a bidi control, a zero-width character, a format character. %20, %2F and the rest of everyday encoding stay silent and unmarked.

Must not fire

An ordinary URL

mailto: sales@example.com?subject=Order 4192&body=Please send a quote

Single spaces throughout, no escapes worth naming, no chip. If this ever shows a warning the whole system is worthless — which is why both rules above are written to be narrow rather than cautious.

4 · Phone numbers: don't reformat them

Pick

Exactly as stored, tabular digits

tel: +4780012345
tel: +47 800 12 345

Two different stored strings, shown as two different strings — because they are. Reformatting would change the thing the visitor is checking, and this page exists so that what is on screen is what is stored. font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums is the free part: every digit takes the same width, so the number reads evenly without anyone regrouping it.

Against

Grouped by country

tel: +47 800 12 345

Nicer, and three separate problems. It needs per-country metadata — the libphonenumber tables, which are large and go stale. Country codes are one to three digits with no way to tell which without that table. And tel: bodies carry more than digits: extensions (;ext=), ;phone-context= for local numbers, letters in vanity numbers. The deciding one is none of those: grouping digits makes two different numbers look alike, and looking alike is the attack this whole page defends against.
