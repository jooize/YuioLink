YuioLink · design note 6

Icons, the alignment bug, and lid copy

Two things you were right about, and I was wrong about the cause of one. The symbol was never misaligned by 1px — .msg is a <p>, and the sheet's global paragraph margin was pushing the text down while the <svg> stayed put. My "fix" last round adjusted the symbol, which is the half that was already correct. Zeroing margin on .msg is the actual repair.

The · never rendered because there wasn't one: I described the lid with a middot in prose but built it with a flex gap. It is a real element now — drawn as a dot, so it cannot go missing from a UI font.

And you are right about the TTL. I proposed cutting it to ~2 minutes on the grounds that it only spans a redirect. It does — but a stalled radio, a captive portal, or a phone that sleeps mid-hop can stretch that hop arbitrarily, and the failure mode is a visitor whose use was already spent seeing 410 for content they never got. That is the worst outcome on the site. The 10 minutes costs nothing now that the cookie is deleted on use. Leave TTL_SECS alone.

2 · Lid copy: sentence case, then the action

"Show…" over "Proceed". "Proceed" is what you press to go, and this lid does not go anywhere — it uncovers a button. And the ellipsis is the site's own established convention: the history head already uses "Clear…" for exactly this, a control that opens further controls rather than acting. Apple's rule is the same — a trailing ellipsis means more is required before anything happens.

4 · The arrow: what the site already means by it

Your proposed rule — plain arrow for internal, boxed arrow for external — isn't the web's convention, and more importantly isn't this site's. The real axis in common use is does it open a new tab. And app.js already draws one of these: openIcon, commented "the arrow out of a box's corner: opens elsewhere", used on the history row's target="_blank" link. That gives the boxed arrow a settled meaning here — new tab — and Continue is a same-tab navigation.

Pick

Bare arrow

Same tab, another site Continue to example.com

The most-used external-link mark on the web, and it stays clear of the boxed arrow the history row has already claimed for "new tab". Amber is what says "you leave"; the arrow just confirms the direction.

If new tab

Boxed arrow

Only if Continue gains target="_blank" Continue to example.com

Correct if the button opens a new tab — but it shouldn't. A visitor followed a link to get somewhere; leaving a spent preview behind in the old tab is clutter, and the back button is the thing that makes an interstitial tolerable.

Also fine

No glyph

Colour and label carry it Continue to example.com

The label already names another site and the colour already means "you leave". Against: the arrow is the one mark that says link, not button — which is the whole capability change, and the reason right-click now works.

6 · On blue in the URL — it is already there

Worth knowing before changing it: .pv-url .reg already carries the accent, as a 13% blue highlight behind bold near-black text, not as blue text. That was the right call and I would keep it. The registrable domain is the single most security-critical string on the page, so it wants maximum contrast; blue text would trade legibility for decoration, and — on a page whose entire job is teaching people to read a URL — it would make the domain look clickable when it deliberately is not.

Today

Accent highlight, dark text

https://example.com/products/keycaps

The domain is marked and maximally readable. Everything else on the line is graded by importance: scheme tertiary, path and query secondary.

Blue text

Domain in accent

https://example.com/products/keycaps

Reads as a hyperlink inside a block whose whole point is that it is text you inspect, not a control you press — and blue is the colour this site uses for "stays on YuioLink", which is the opposite of what this string represents.
