Shown and stored — the hero, the escapes, and where the record lives

The three questions this note answers. Should the hero write out the %-escapes? Is there a way to remove "Exactly as Stored"? Or should it collapse by default unless it earns its line? Reading the code first turned up something that reframes all three: the hero already writes out every escape that matters. The decode policy in urlview::decode_for_reading is per character:
StoredThe hero showsDress
%20a spacedotted underline (.dsp) — the receipt
%26 %3D %23 %25 (& = # %)the escape, rawdim — decoding would redraw the URL's own structure
escape of an invisible / direction-changing characterthe escape, rawred, with the Hidden Characters chip
a literal invisible characteran escape we inventred, with the chip — the hero out-honests the stored string
padding spacesthe spacestinted, with the Padded With Spaces chip
everything else (%C3%A9→é, %3A%2F%2F→://)the decoded characterplain — the only silent decode, and the only job the exact line still has
So the real question is narrow: what should carry the provenance of that last row — a duplicate line (today), raw escapes in the hero (B), a receipt in the hero (C), or the fold (D)? Sections 1–5 show each on the same three links.
Found while reading, worth fixing regardless of the verdict:
  • The hero's path drops its dress. path_markup and path_slices_markup flatten pieces to text before splitting on / — a dotted space, a red escape, or padding in the path loses its colour in the hero and in the slice rows. The characters survive; the marking does not.
  • The embedded domain is washed twice. uri_value_pieces promises "no accent wash — spent once per page", but .pv-url .reg matches the Piece::Domain inside a query value too, so a carried address's domain gets the wash and the 1.06em bump in the hero. Section 1, middle card, shows it live.
  • .pv-url .dl is unstyled. A carried address's :// renders at full value-strength in the hero instead of receding; the same piece is dim in the slices and the raw line.
  • An invalid-UTF-8 escape leaks a replacement mark. The comment in decode_for_reading says it "stays escaped", but the piece match falls through to Piece::Text("�") — the raw escape is lost from display and decoded_differs fires on a substitution the reader cannot see.

2 · Option B — write every escape out in the hero, retire the exact line

everyday decodes — the price
https://example.com/files/r%C3%A9sum%C3%A9.pdf?q=hello%20world

The hero becomes the record — and stops being readable. résumé.pdf is now a puzzle. Every non-ASCII letter, every space, in every everyday link, forever. The one virtue: nothing left to prove, so the exact line really can go.

a carried address — the price, where it hurts
https://login.example.co.uk/reset?next=https%3A%2F%2Fexample-mail.com%2F

The warning loses its evidence. "Carries Another Address" would chip a value the eye can no longer parse as an address — no ://, no bold domain. The card would be asserting something it just stopped showing. And on the hidden-character card B changes nothing at all: the dangerous escapes are already written out today. B pays its whole price on the harmless links and buys nothing on the dangerous ones. Rejected.

3 · Option C — decode for reading, but every decoded run wears the receipt

everyday decodes — self-declaring
https://example.com/files/résumé.pdf?q=hello world

The dotted underline already means this. .dsp is documented as "dotted = it was an escape, dim %NN = it still is one, bare = it really is that character" — spaces are just the only run that got the dress. Generalising it (.dec, same one-line style) makes the hero say that something was encoded without saying the bytes. Readable and honest.

a carried address — the disguise is outlined
https://login.example.co.uk/reset?next=https://example-mail.com/

The receipt lands exactly on the disguised characters. The :// and trailing / were stored as escapes; the domain was stored in the clear. The dotting shows precisely which characters were dressed up — the shape of the trick, in the hero itself. Cost: piece provenance must survive merging and structure_value's re-parse, and the path-flattening fix becomes a prerequisite. Worth it, but it is a follow-up, not the fix.

The full stack. Hero: readable, with the disguised characters dotted. Chip: the claim. Fold (forced open): the removable part and the record proving the claim. Nothing is said twice at the same register, and nothing true is more than one glance away.

Verdict.
  • Do D now. Move exact_line inside the fold in slice_section (http/https only; mailto, tel, sms keep their always-on record — their heroes are true renderings). The gates already exist and already agree: the fold exists whenever the record does, and warnings force it open. The everyday %20 link gets its quiet page back; no warned card loses anything.
  • B is rejected — it pays on the harmless cards and buys nothing on the dangerous ones, which already write their escapes out.
  • C is the follow-up, not the fix: generalise the .dsp receipt to all decoded runs (.dec). It needs piece provenance to survive merging and structure_value, and the path-flattening fix first. With C in place the hero really does show everything the reader needs; the record keeps only two jobs — the bytes, and the Copy pill.
  • Fix the four findings (path dress flattening, double wash, unstyled .pv-url .dl, the invalid-UTF-8 leak) regardless.
