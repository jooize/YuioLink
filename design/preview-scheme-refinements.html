YuioLink · design note 5

Refinements: alignment, http, "Proceed", and the blue question

A correction to round 4. I wrote that a tel: link is "one accidental tap from being dialled". That overstates it: iOS Safari interposes a confirmation sheet, and Android generally opens the dialer pre-filled rather than placing the call. The premium-rate risk is real, but a browser confirmation usually stands between the tap and the charge — so the cover is a second seatbelt, not the only one. That changes how hard the cover has to work, and it is why section 4 below proposes covering fewer schemes than round 4 did.

1 · The symbol, aligned

Was

Nudged by margin

No Button for This OneThe address stored here would tell your browser to do something rather than go somewhere, so YuioLink shows it and stops there.

A 1px margin against an implicit line box. The glyph sits high, and the error grows with the type scale.

Now

Locked to the heading's line box

No Button for This OneThe address stored here would tell your browser to do something rather than go somewhere, so YuioLink shows it and stops there.

The heading gets an explicit line-height: 19px and the symbol box is given that same height with place-items: center. They now share an optical centre at any size, with no magic numbers to re-tune.

2 · Marking insecure http

Worth doing — this is the one place YuioLink knows something the visitor's browser will only tell them after they've arrived. The constraint: colour must never be the only carrier, or the warning vanishes for a colourblind reader.

Colour only

Red scheme, nothing else

http://example.com/page Continue to example.com

Reads as decoration to anyone who doesn't already know the convention, and disappears entirely for the readers most likely to need it. Red also collides with the lookalike-domain panel, which is a genuinely adversarial warning.

Pick

Red scheme + a chip that says it

http://example.com/page
Not Encrypted
Continue to example.com

The colour draws the eye, the words carry the meaning, the open padlock carries it a third way. "Not Encrypted" over "Not Secure": it is the precise claim, and it doesn't imply we have judged the site itself — which we haven't, and say so two lines below.

Softer

Chip only, scheme left dim

http://example.com/page
Not Encrypted
Continue to example.com

Keeps the URL rendering untouched — one fewer colour in a line that is already doing part-by-part colouring. Against: the chip floats free of the thing it describes; tying red to the four characters that are the problem is the clearer teach.

3 · The lid, in title case

"— lift to continue" is out. Each lid states the consequence, then the word you press.

Pick

Consequence · Proceed

mailto:
hello@example.com
Opens Your Mail App Proceed Write to hello@example.com

The lid names what happens; "Proceed" with a chevron is unmistakably the control. Two registers, one line.

Barest

Consequence only

magnet:
?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3…9ab4
Hands This to a Torrent App Open in Your Torrent App

Cleanest line, but nothing says it is pressable except the hatching. On a phone, where there is no hover, that is a real gap.

Verb-first

Proceed · consequence

tel:
+47 800 12 345
Proceed — Dials a Number Call +47 800 12 345

Puts the control word first, where the eye lands. Against: it reads as an instruction to proceed, which is advice we don't want to be giving.

4 · Why not blue for mailto and tel?

Because blue on this site means you stay on YuioLink, and a mailto: does not — it hands your address book an addressee. But your instinct that these are not all equally alarming is right, and there is a better lever for it than colour.

Blue

Blue plate and button

mailto:
hello@example.com Write to hello@example.com

It looks calm, and that is the problem: it is the same blue as Reveal Destination, which is the site's promise that pressing keeps you here. Once one blue button launches an external app, the visitor can no longer read colour to know whether they are about to leave — and that inference is the only thing the palette was buying.

Pick

Amber throughout — cover only what commits

mailto:
hello@example.com Write to hello@example.com

Same amber, no cover. mailto:, sms:, xmpp:, irc:, ircs: and matrix: all open a draft — an app appears with a blank waiting for you, and nothing is sent until you send it. Nothing commits, so nothing needs a lid.

The lid stays on tel:, magnet:, ftp: and spotify: — the ones where the app starts doing something on arrival: placing a call, joining a swarm, fetching, playing. Colour keeps saying where you go; the cover now says whether it commits. Two signals, two questions, neither overloaded.

This also settles the round-4 worry honestly. With the correction at the top — browsers usually confirm before dialling — a lid on every non-web scheme was friction charged to six harmless ones to slow down four. Covering only what commits keeps the protection where the risk is.
