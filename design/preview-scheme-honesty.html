YuioLink · design note 8

Say what the scheme is, not what the device will do

You've found the flaw in every lid I've written. "Opens your mail app", "Hands this to another app", "Starts a file transfer" — all four are predictions about a machine we cannot see. A magnet: link on a laptop with no client registered does nothing. A mailto: may open Gmail in a tab, a native client, or a "choose an app" sheet. A site whose entire pitch is "we show you what the link really is" cannot be the one making up the ending. Magnet SVGs binned, as asked.
What we actually know
  • The scheme, exactly as stored.
  • The body after scheme:, verbatim.
  • What the scheme means — it is a published standard.
  • That we will hand it to the browser unchanged.
What we're guessing at
  • Whether anything is registered to handle it.
  • Which app, if any, takes it.
  • Whether that app acts, drafts, or just opens.
  • Whether the OS asks first.

So the description line describes the scheme, which is a fact, and the button says Open, which is all we are actually doing. One hedge line covers the rest, stated once and quietly.

1 · The B / F mix you asked for

B marked the scheme; F gave the body the weight. Three ways to have both — each shown with tel: (short body) and magnet: (a body that punishes big type).

Mix 1

Equal billing

Same size, only the scheme marked
tel: +47 800 12 345
magnet: ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3…9ab4&dn=ubuntu-24.04

Balanced for tel:. Against: two 21px bold objects stacked reads as two headings, and the magnet hash becomes a wall.

Mix 2 · Pick

Body leads, scheme marked above

Scheme 15px, body up to 23px
tel: +47 800 12 345
magnet: ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3…9ab4&dn=ubuntu-24.04

The destination is the thing a person checks, so it takes the size — your F instinct — while the scheme keeps the domain's highlight so it is still marked as the kind. It also mirrors the https preview's shape, where the small dim scheme sits ahead of the emphasised host.

Mix 3

One line, as a URL

Exactly the .pv-url construction
tel:+47 800 12 345
magnet:?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3…9ab4&dn=ubuntu-24.04

The most consistent of the three — a hostless URL rendered as a URL, no new layout at all. Against: a long magnet body wraps around the highlighted scheme and the marking gets lost mid-paragraph.

2 · Where the description goes

Pick

Under the body, above the button

magnet: ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3…9ab4&dn=ubuntu-24.04
A file identified by its hash, not by a location Open magnet: Link What opens it, if anything, is up to your device.

Expires in 6 days

Reads top-down as one sentence: this scheme, this value, this is what that kind of value is, here is the button. The hedge sits after the control, where it qualifies the outcome rather than delaying the read.

Alt

As a second line on the button

magnet: ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3…9ab4&dn=ubuntu-24.04
Open magnet: Link A file identified by its hash What opens it, if anything, is up to your device.

Expires in 6 days

Compact, and the explanation travels with the control. Against: we removed the two-line button once already because it made the press feel heavier — and a neutral fact does not belong inside an amber control.

Alt

Beside the scheme

magnet: a file identified by its hash ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3…9ab4&dn=ubuntu-24.04
Open magnet: Link What opens it, if anything, is up to your device.

Expires in 6 days

Glosses the scheme right where it appears, like a dictionary entry. Against: mixes proportional and mono type on one line and breaks badly on a phone, where the two halves wrap apart.

3 · What the button says

Pick

Uniform: Open scheme: Link

Open mailto: Link Open tel: Link Open magnet: Link Open ftp: Link

Claims exactly what we do and nothing more. The scheme keeps its lowercase because it is an identifier, not a word — the same rule the rest of the codebase follows. One template, no per-scheme copy to get wrong, and it never lies on a device we have not met.

Hybrid

Colloquial where the spec is explicit

Write to hello@example.com Call +47 800 12 345 Open magnet: Link Open ftp: Link

Friendlier, and you're right that "Call +47 800 12 345" is the clearest thing that line could say — RFC 3966 defines tel: as a phone number, so the noun is safe even if the verb is a guess. Against: it splits the family in two, and a visitor cannot tell why some buttons speak plainly and others don't.

Barest

Just "Open Link"

Open Link Open Link Open Link Open Link

Nothing to disprove. Against: the button is the last thing read before a decision, and this one carries no information at all — everything it might have said is now several lines above it.

4 · Do we still want the lid?

The lid was justified by "this commits something". We just agreed we don't know that. So the honest question is whether not knowing is itself worth a second click.

Pick · No lid

One press, everything said

tel: +47 800 12 345
A phone number Open tel: Link What opens it, if anything, is up to your device.

The visitor already has the scheme, the value, what that kind of value is, and an explicit statement that we don't know the ending — more than any browser gives them. A cover on top of that is friction with no claim left behind it, and its own label would have to be a guess. It also drops a <details>, a stacked grid and a 3D transform from the stylesheet.

Keep it

Cover the unknown

tel: +47 800 12 345
A phone number
Leaves the browserShow… Open tel: Link

"Leaves the browser" is the one lid label that survives the honesty test — it is true of every non-web scheme and predicts nothing. For: a mis-tap on a phone is cheap to make and, for tel:, potentially expensive to undo. Against: it taxes mailto: — far and away the most common of these — to slow down a case that is rare and that the OS usually confirms anyway.

5 · Every description

Each is a statement about the scheme's own definition, checkable against its RFC. None mentions an app, a device, or an outcome.

And the hedge, once, under the button: "What opens it, if anything, is up to your device." The "if anything" is the load-bearing half — it is the only line on the page that prepares a visitor for the very common case where pressing appears to do nothing at all.
