YuioLink · design note 3

Badge wording, and a refusal that doesn't shout

Settled from round 2: no subline (the button stays one line), "Reveal Destination" kept, badge stays below the button in the One-Time purple, and the multi-use code paths stay for a future where a link can open more than once.

1 · What the pill should say

Since multi-use is coming back, the pair has to survive N > 1. Each option is shown as the one-time case beside the case it grows into.

Today

Opens Once / Limited Use

A name for one, a category for the other
Opens Once Limited Use

"Opens Once" is excellent — concrete, and it answers the question a visitor actually has. "Limited Use" answers nothing: limited to what? It is the only badge on the site that describes a category instead of a behaviour, and a visitor cannot act on it.

Your idea

Reveals Once / Limited Reveals

Borrows the button's verb
Reveals Once Limited Reveals

More precise, and you are right that it is. What the limit governs is the reveal, not the opening — once revealed, that visitor can follow the destination as often as they like. Against: "reveals" is our verb for our mechanism, and it makes the link the actor ("the link reveals") when the visitor is. "Limited Reveals" also inherits the vagueness of "Limited Use".

Pick

Opens Once / Opens 3 Times

One sentence, N substituted
Opens Once Opens 3 Times

The one-time badge stops being a special word and becomes the N=1 case of a single template — which is exactly what a future multi-use needs, with no new vocabulary to invent. It is also the only option that tells a visitor a number, and the number is the link's total, never its remainder, so it discloses nothing about whether someone got there first.

Also good

One Reveal / 3 Reveals

Count, in the button's verb
One Reveal 3 Reveals

Combines the precision of your version with the count of the pick above, and pairs tightly with the button directly over it. Against: a bare noun-phrase is a label where the others are statements, and "One Reveal" is measurably weaker than "Opens Once" at conveying finality — it sounds like an allowance, not a limit.

Worth noting either way: the creator's word for this is "One-Time" (the create picker, the stats page). The badge deliberately says something else because it addresses a different person — the creator picks a type, the visitor needs a behaviour. That split holds for multi-use too: the picker would say "Limited", the badge would say "Opens 3 Times".

3 · The refusal, without the red box

The red fill was borrowed from .pv-idn, where it belongs — a lookalike domain is an active deception aimed at you. This is a stored value failing validation: serious, but not an attack in progress, and nothing bad can happen from here because the button is simply gone. Same content, four temperatures.

Pick

C · Native alert

Symbol carries the colour; the surface stays neutral javascript:alert(1)

No button for this oneThe address stored here would tell your browser to do something rather than go somewhere, so YuioLink shows it and stops there.

This is how macOS states a refusal: a symbol, a bold headline, a secondary line — on the ordinary surface, not a coloured one. The red is concentrated in the one element small enough to carry it without shouting, and the headline does the work the fill was doing.

Minimal

A · The page's own voice

No box, same as .pv-caution javascript:alert(1) No button for this one. The address stored here would tell your browser to do something rather than go somewhere, so YuioLink shows it and stops there.

Nothing new enters the stylesheet — this is exactly the treatment the page already gives its cautions. Against: it reads as one more advisory line on a page that already has two, when it is actually reporting that something is wrong.

Neutral

B · Quiet glass

Field surface, hairline edge, no colour at all javascript:alert(1)

No button for this one. The address stored here would tell your browser to do something rather than go somewhere, so YuioLink shows it and stops there.

The most "Liquid Glass" of the four and the calmest. Against: with no colour and no symbol it is indistinguishable at a glance from a help panel, and this one deserves to be noticed.

Today

D · Red fill

Borrowed from the lookalike-domain panel javascript:alert(1)

YuioLink won't open this. The address stored for this name isn't a web address — it's an instruction for your browser, which a link should never be.

Spends the page's loudest signal on its rarest state, and devalues it for the lookalike-domain warning, which is the one a visitor will actually meet and the one where getting it wrong costs them something.

4 · Four ways to word it

All shown in treatment C. The constraint that shapes them: the allowlist is broad (http, https, mailto, tel, sms, ftp, magnet, spotify, xmpp, irc, matrix), so no sentence may claim this is "not a web address" — mailto: isn't either, and it is perfectly welcome.

Pick

P1 · Do something / go somewhere

No button for this oneThe address stored here would tell your browser to do something rather than go somewhere, so YuioLink shows it and stops there.

Draws the line where the code draws it, in words with no jargon in them. "Shows it and stops there" states the whole policy in five words, and the headline answers the question the visitor is actually asking — where is my button.

Tightest

P2 · Shown, not linked

Shown, not linkedThis address isn't one YuioLink will hand to a browser. You can read it above exactly as it was stored — we just won't make it clickable.

Shortest of the four and the headline is almost a slogan. Against: "won't hand to a browser" is a mechanism a visitor has no model for, and it never says why.

Protective

P3 · We didn't decide for you

This one we won't click for youWhat's stored under this name isn't a place — it's something for a browser to run. It's printed above, exactly as stored, so you can see what was here and judge it yourself.

The most in-character for a site whose whole premise is "we show you what the link is and you decide". Against: "we won't click it for you" is a little arch, and the longest of the four.

Blunt

P4 · Something is wrong here

This link shouldn't existYuioLink refuses addresses like this one when a link is created, so finding it here means something went wrong. It's printed above, unchanged, and there's deliberately nothing to press.

The only version that is honest about the anomaly — this state is unreachable through the front door, so a visitor seeing it has found a bug or a tampered row. Against: "shouldn't exist" invites alarm the visitor can do nothing with, and quietly admits a compromise to whoever caused it.
