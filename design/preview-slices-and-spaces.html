Slices and spaces

Dropping "to" pulls a thread, and the whole label problem unravels nicely. If the addresses need no label — and they don't; an email address announces itself — then recipients are not table rows at all: they are what the link is, so they move up into the headline, as one comma-joined text run identical to the stored list (selection copies it unbroken, the spotify trick). The table keeps only the URL's own keyed parameters, ?cc and &subject, which self-describe. That leaves exactly three labels in the whole design — user, port, path in the https fold — and section 2 shows four ways to dress them, including your grey-background pill. My pick is D: stop labelling and start slicing — every row is a verbatim slice of the URL (alice@, :8443, /reset), and our word moves to the right edge as a small italic gloss. The mono column is then 100% stored characters top to bottom, and the gloss is unmistakably ours — which is also your "right side" idea from last round, landed where it works: on short slices, not on long values.

And yes on shading the two sides of the @. Round 16 made both sides bold, which read as noise and quietly diluted the site rule that bold means a registrable domain. Now: local part regular weight, full colour; @ dim; domain bold. Each side different, nothing dimmed to grey, and the bold rule is pure again.

3 · A space, or a %20?

The reader can't tell — so the space that was an escape keeps a shadow of it. A space produced by decoding %20 wears a faint dotted underline; a space that is literally in the stored string (possible only in mailto: / tel: / sms: — the https canonicaliser never lets one survive) is bare. Suspicious runs and edge-spaces keep their danger tint as before, and the exact line stays the arbiter of record. The dot-line is deliberately the quietest mark on the card: it answers the question when you ask it and vanishes when you don't. One more corner pinned down: %25 is one of the four structure escapes we never decode, so a visible %20 in decoded text simply cannot occur — dotted space means "was %20", visible dim %NN means "still an escape", bare space means "really a space". Three appearances, three meanings, no overlap.
