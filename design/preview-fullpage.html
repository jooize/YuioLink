YuioLink · design note 11

Full pages, and two findings

1 · The Public Suffix List is already handled. urlview.rs calls psl::domain_str, so bbc.co.uk highlights as bbc.co.uk, not co.uk — and a host with no recognised public suffix (an IP, an intranet name) falls back to highlighting the whole host. Nothing to fix.

2 · Spaces can reach the page, and only in one place. I ran the canonicalizer over a set of hostile inputs. Everything invisible arrives percent-encoded and therefore visible — U+202E (right-to-left override) becomes %E2%80%AE, U+200B (zero-width space) becomes %E2%80%8B, and the stored form is always pure ASCII. But a literal ASCII space survives in the opaque body of a cannot-be-a-base URL:

mailto:hello world@example.com → mailto:hello world@example.com
tel:+47 800 12 345 → tel:+47 800 12 345
https://example.com/a b → https://example.com/a%20b (encoded)

So mailto: and tel: are the only places a run of spaces can pad a destination out of view. That is worth marking — section 4. (The probe was a throwaway test; it has been reverted.)

1 · The scheme should not wear the domain's mark

You're right, and it gets sharper now that the body carries a highlighted domain of its own: under the accent-highlight version, one card shows the same wash twice, meaning two different things. The rule worth keeping is that the accent wash appears exactly once on a page, on the registrable domain.

Was

Accent highlight

mailto: hello@example.com

Two washes, two meanings. The scheme is not who you are dealing with.

Pick

Plain, no mark

mailto: hello@example.com

At 24px mono bold it is already the largest object on the page — size is its emphasis, and it needs no second one.

Neutral chip

mailto: hello@example.com

Reads as a tag rather than a claim. Still one enclosure more than the page needs.

Recede to secondary

mailto: hello@example.com

Goes too far — the scheme is the reason this page looks different, and it is now the quietest thing on it.

3 · Full page — magnet, nothing truncated

Every character of the destination, always. The hash wraps rather than eliding, which is the only honest option on a page whose promise is that you can see what you are getting.

YuioLink Redirect calmFALCON yuio.link
magnet: ?xt=urn:btih:c12fe3a94b81d7e05f2c6a9048bb3e1d7f4a2c60&dn=ubuntu-24.04-desktop-amd64.iso&tr=udp://tracker.example.org:6969
Show… A file identified by its hash Open Link · magnet: A file identified by its hash
What opens it, if anything, is up to your device.

Expires in 6 days

YuioLinks expire and are reused, so a link can point somewhere else later. Always check the destination.

The grading is what makes 200 characters survivable: xt, dn and tr recede as labels, the hash sits at full weight, and dn — the only human-readable claim a magnet makes about itself — is readable at a glance. A tracker host gets no accent wash: it is not who you are dealing with, the hash is.

4 · The two hazards worth marking

Padded with spaces
YuioLink Redirect quickHERON yuio.link
mailto: billing@example.com           attacker@evil.test
Show… An email address Write to this address An email address
What opens it, if anything, is up to your device.

Expires in 6 days

The run of spaces is tinted rather than replaced, so ⌘C still copies the real string — the mark is a property of the display, not of the text. Two highlighted domains on one card is itself the tell: a legitimate mailto: has one. Note the button label falls back to "Write to this address" when the body is not a single clean address.

Stuffed with escapes
YuioLink Redirect plainMARTEN yuio.link
mailto: hello@example.com?subject=Invoice%E2%80%AEfdp.exe
Show… An email address Write to hello@example.com An email address
What opens it, if anything, is up to your device.

Expires in 6 days

Canonicalization already did the hard part — the right-to-left override arrives as %E2%80%AE instead of silently reversing the text. Tinting %XX runs just says a character was hidden here, which is the one thing the encoded form does not make obvious.
