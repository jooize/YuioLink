YuioLink · design note 26 · interactive — tap the dotted space, the red escape, the dim escape

Touch the receipt — what a marked character says when asked

The idea (user's, 2026-08-19). The hero marks three kinds of character — dotted space (was an escape), dim %NN (still is one), red %NN (hides something) — and the marks say that without saying what. Tap one and it should explain itself. Ground rules that shaped both variants:
  • No tooltips. Touch has no hover and screen readers lose floating text — the codebase already refuses tooltips for exactly this (see flashSource).
  • Mobile first. A space glyph is a ~4px target; the hit area grows via padding pulled back with negative margin (.mark-tap), keeping ~40px touch height without moving the line.
  • No JS, no loss. The marks stay what they are today — receipts — and the fold still tells everything. The tap is purely additive.
  • Trusted types. Captions are set with textContent; no markup is assembled (the contract-lint tests already pin this for preview.js).

The explanation surface already exists — the tap just aims the reader at it: the fold opens if closed, and the mark's own slice row and its bytes in the record pulse once. Strength: everything shown is in context, nothing new to dismiss. Weakness: on a phone the fold opening moves the page under the finger, and the pulse answers "where" better than it answers "what" — the reader still has to infer that %E2%80%8B means an invisible character.

One stable line, no occlusion, reader stays put. The caption appears in a reserved spot under the hero, so nothing floats over the URL and nothing reflows mid-read on a phone. It answers "what" in words the chip never has room for; a red mark keeps its red in the caption. Tapping the same mark again — or another mark — replaces or clears the line; aria-live="polite" reads it out. The dim structural escapes (%26) join in for free, which quietly teaches what the three dresses mean.

Verdict (proposed).
  • B is the feature: it answers the actual question ("what does this represent?") at the place the eye already is, works one-handed on a phone, and degrades to nothing — no JS, no captions, no loss.
  • A is not worth its jump. The fold is already one tap away and opens itself whenever a warning fires; auto-opening it from a character tap moves the page under the reader's finger for little gain. Drop it.
  • Every marked run (.dsp, .bad, .pe/.pct) gets the same treatment, in the hero and in the slice rows — one rule, no special cases.
  • Real implementation: the spans become focusable (tabindex, role="button", per-mark aria-label), captions are server-written into a data-what attribute so preview.js only moves text it never composes.
