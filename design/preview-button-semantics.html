YuioLink · design note

Preview buttons: colour, element, cost

Four buttons appear on link pages. They differ along three axes that are currently carried by one signal (colour). This note separates the axes and proposes which ones deserve their own visible treatment.

1 · The colour rule, stated

Colour answers exactly one question: where do I end up? Nothing else is encoded in it — not the kind of link, not the cost, not the risk.

Blue — --accent

You stay on YuioLink. It is the site's own accent, used for every action whose result is another YuioLink page. Reveal spends a use, but it lands you here, on a page we render and stand behind.

Amber — #ff9f0a

You leave for somewhere we don't vouch for. Amber is the one break in the palette, and it earns it: crossing to a stranger's site is the only irreversible, un-undoable thing this site offers. Dark text, because white cannot reach 4.5:1 on amber — this pairing is ~8:1.

Red — rejected

Not used. Red asserts the destination is malicious. We don't know that, and a warning that fires on every single redirect is a warning nobody reads by week two. Amber says "check this", which is the true claim.

2 · The four situations

What differs between them is not only colour: it is what the control is (a link you can copy, or a form that changes server state) and what pressing it costs. Today both of those are invisible.

4 · Three ways to make the difference visible

Each variant is shown as the full set, so the family reads together rather than one button at a time.

A · Glyph only

Anchors carry ↗. Buttons carry nothing. Continue to example.com

Adds: the one axis colour can't carry — "this is a link, you can take it with you". Costs: nothing; ↗ is the most conventional glyph on the web. Silent on: the cost of Reveal, which the badge below already names.

B · Glyph + cost line

Reveal states its price on the button itself. Continue to example.com

Adds: the price is where the decision is, not below it, and the one-time case reads as a sentence instead of a badge. Costs: the "Opens Once" / "Limited Use" badge under the button becomes a repeat — it would have to go, and it is currently the only place the link's shape is named.

C · Glyph + cost + live domain

The domain inside the label is marked as the thing you're going to. Continue to example.com

Adds: nothing a user acts on. Costs: a dotted underline on a domain is what a phishing page does to look legitimate, and it competes with the full URL printed directly above. Recommended against.

Recommendation: A, or B if the cost line survives dropping the badge. The ↗ is the honest addition — it marks a genuine capability change (this control is now copyable and openable in a new tab) rather than restating what colour already says. The cost line in B is a real improvement to the limited-link decision, but it duplicates the badge; adopting it means deleting .pv-badge, and that badge is also what tells a visitor the link is one-time before they read any button.

Not proposed: a third colour, an icon on the Reveal buttons (an eye reads as "peek", which is wrong — the view is spent whether or not you look), and any treatment that makes Continue less prominent than Reveal. Continue is the expected outcome of visiting a link; the interstitial already taxes it with a click.
