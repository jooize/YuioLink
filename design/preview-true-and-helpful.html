YuioLink · design note 18

True and helpful

Your closing line is the whole design: true to the contents and as helpful as possible — that is exactly the three-register rule (headline reads, table explains, exact line proves), and this round is it arriving at the last holdouts.
  • Yes — the table now speaks the same syntax language as the URL line and the exact line: accent delimiters, bold domains and local parts, dim residual escapes, red weird ones. One palette, three registers.
  • The URL line decodes its escapes — %3A%2F%2F becomes :// — except the four characters that would redraw the URL's structure (& = # %, kept escaped and dim) and anything invisible or direction-changing (kept escaped and red, with its chip). And because the line may now differ from what is stored, https gets the conditional exact line: it appears, and takes ⌘C, only when decoding actually changed something. An everyday URL shows nothing new.
  • No green https:// — argued below. Yes red username@, paired with its chip.
  • Our invented labels change voice, not colour: to, user, port, path are set in the UI font, italic — the voice the card already uses when we speak (the "Exactly as stored" label, the notes). Mono = the stored string talking; sans italic = us talking. The second recipient also wears its true comma: , to.
  • The Spotify selection bug is a block boundary, and the fix is to remove it: scheme and body become one inline text run that soft-wraps. Soft wraps are not characters — selecting across both lines now copies spotify:track:6rqh… with no break and no space. Bonus: when such a headline is character-identical to what is stored, the exact line has nothing to prove and disappears (spotify, xmpp).
Why not green for https://. Green is a verdict, and it would be ours. The scheme being https says the transport is encrypted — it says nothing about who is on the other end; every phishing site worth its salt has a padlock. The palette's precedent is careful about this: red appears only where we know something is wrong (a lookalike domain, a hidden character, plain http), amber means "you are leaving", and nothing in the design ever says "this is safe". A green https:// would say it on every page, and it would be the one claim on the card we cannot stand behind. Encryption is the norm; its absence is the signal — that is why "Not Encrypted" exists and "Encrypted" does not.

Red username@, on the other hand, is exactly right — same reasoning, opposite direction. Userinfo in a shared web link is almost always either a trick (https://bbc.co.uk@evil.example/) or a leak, we detect it mechanically, and the red characters co-fire with the "Username in the Address" chip the way hidden characters co-fire with theirs. Yellow/amber would dilute the button language ("amber = you leave"); red already means "look here, something is off".

The break you copied was a block boundary, not a character. The scheme plate and the value were two elements, and element boundaries put a hard newline into the selection. Now they are one inline text run that soft-wraps at the <wbr> after the colon — soft wraps are layout, not text, so selecting both lines pastes spotify:track:6rqhFgbbKwnb9MLmUQDhG6, no break, no space, into anything. The card looks all but identical. And since this headline is now character-identical to the stored string, the exact line has nothing to prove and is gone — same for xmpp: when its line matches. The formatted headlines (tel, mailto) keep theirs: there the rendering really does differ, and the proof stays.
