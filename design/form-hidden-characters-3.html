Hidden characters in the form, round 3

Round 2's D, grown with History's chip. The field always shows ticks. The chip under it cycles the way History's does, and Labels brings in the marked copy only when you ask for it, so the text is not drawn twice by default.

  • Ticks (the start): a red wedge under each hidden character, inside the field, kept in place while you type, on every line. Click just under a character's wedge (the pointer turns into a hand) to open its entry. The caret still goes where you clicked.
  • Labels: the marked copy under the field, with History's labels. Click a label to open its entry. The ticks stay in the field.
  • Removed: the characters leave the field, and the chip turns green. Click again to put them back, or use ⌘Z.
  • A redirect is drawn in the copy as it will be stored: a hidden character in the path, query or fragment becomes its escape (%E2%80%8D) in the URL card's reverse red. In the domain it turns the whole name into punycode.

Should History use the wedge too?

History's Ticks mode today draws a 3px bar beside each character, which takes width and pushes the text aside a little. The wedge takes no width, so the row reads exactly as the text does, and one tick shape would mean the same thing everywhere. Its catch: History's 19px lines leave little room under the text, so the wedge is a size smaller there. Both rows hold the same multiline text.
